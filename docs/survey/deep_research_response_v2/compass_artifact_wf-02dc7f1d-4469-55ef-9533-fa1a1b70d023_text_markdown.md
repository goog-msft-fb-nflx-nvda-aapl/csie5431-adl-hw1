# Pushing Past a Strong Baseline: Multi-Label Sales-Intent Classification (Round 2)

## TL;DR
- The single highest-expected-value move is **LP-FT (linear-probe-then-fine-tune, Kumar et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.10054) combined with the Mosbach/Zhang stability recipe** (debiased AdamW + warmup, re-initializing the top 1–6 encoder layers, and training far longer than the apparent plateau); this directly targets your two confirmed findings — extreme LR sensitivity on the 627-example English route and the fact that a randomly-initialized head on top of a strong embedding model (BGE-M3) distorts good pretrained features on small data.
- For the rare-class bottleneck (`Too_Expensive`, `Compare_Competitor`), move from Asymmetric Loss to **Distribution-Balanced Loss (Wu et al. 2020) / the CB-NTR variant from Huang et al. EMNLP 2021** (co-occurrence-aware re-weighting + negative-tolerant regularization), which was designed for exactly long-tailed *multi-label text* and is a ~30-line plain-PyTorch change; pair it with **translate-train** (MarianMT/NLLB translating your ~1900 Chinese examples into English) rather than the back-translation you already found fails.
- Reimplementing **SetFit-style contrastive fine-tuning in plain PyTorch is feasible and low-risk** (it is ~100 lines: cosine-embedding pairs loss on BGE-M3's CLS embeddings, then a logistic-regression head), and is the best-motivated "stronger paradigm than plain AutoModelForSequenceClassification" for using an embedding model on small data — but note several candidate embedding models (jina-v3, Qwen3-Embedding, mE5-instruct) were trained on classification data and are compliance-risky under your rule; **BGE-M3 and LaBSE are clean.**

## Key Findings

1. **Your LR-sensitivity finding is a textbook symptom, and the literature prescribes specific fixes beyond "try more LRs":** debiased Adam (not BERTAdam), a warmup phase, top-layer re-initialization, and training much longer with early stopping (Mosbach et al. 2021, arXiv:2006.04884; Zhang et al. 2021, arXiv:2006.05987). You have already independently rediscovered the "train longer than the plateau" result.

2. **The BGE-M3-as-classifier question has a principled answer:** because BGE-M3 is a strong retrieval embedder, a randomly-initialized classification head will "distort" its features during full fine-tuning (Kumar et al. 2022). LP-FT and SetFit-style contrastive tuning both preserve those features and are the right paradigms; plain `AutoModelForSequenceClassification` is the weakest of the three on small data.

3. **Translate-train is well-established and consistently beats zero-shot / translation-free transfer in resource-imbalanced setups** (XTREME, Hu et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.11080; MultiATIS++ trans-train results), and is a genuinely different intervention from the back-translation you found fails twice — it adds *new content*, not paraphrases of existing English.

4. **For rare classes, purpose-built multi-label long-tail losses exist and are validated on text** (Huang et al., EMNLP 2021, aclanthology 2021.emnlp-main.643, with open PyTorch code), dominating plain BCE/focal on Macro-F1 for tail labels — a strictly better-motivated lever than ASL for your two weak classes.

5. **Reliable model selection on your ~117-example English dev slice requires multi-seed + k-fold**, not single-run dev comparison; the instability literature shows single-seed dev F1 on datasets this small has variance large enough to invert config rankings (Dodge et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.06305).

6. **Among candidate embedding models, several are compliance-risky** under your "pretrained LM must not have been trained on another intent dataset" rule: jina-embeddings-v3 ships a dedicated classification LoRA adapter, Qwen3-Embedding was trained on synthetic classification data, and mE5-instruct was fine-tuned on E5-mistral data that includes classification-style tasks. **BGE-M3 (retrieval-only) and LaBSE (bitext-only) are clean.**

## Details

### 1. Few-shot / small-N fine-tuning paradigms for an embedding model

**SetFit (Tunstall et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.11055; code github.com/huggingface/setfit).** SetFit fine-tunes a Sentence Transformer body contrastively on sampled sentence *pairs* (positives = same label, negatives = different label) using a cosine/Siamese objective, then trains a lightweight classification head (scikit-learn logistic regression or a small torch head) on the resulting embeddings. It matches PET/PEFT accuracy with orders of magnitude fewer parameters and is an order of magnitude faster to train, and the authors show it works multilingually simply by swapping the ST body. On the RAFT few-shot benchmark, per Tunstall et al. 2022, "SetFit_RoBERTa outperforms GPT3 and PET by 8.6 and 1.7 points respectively… [and] surpasses the human baseline in 7 out of 11 tasks," while being "more than 30 times smaller than T-Few 11B." (For scale on that T-Few baseline: per Liu et al., arXiv:2205.05638, T-Few "attains a state-of-the-art accuracy of 75.8% and outperforms the human baseline (73.5% accuracy)… The next-best model… achieves 6% lower accuracy and GPT-3 175B attains only 62.7%.")

*Plain-PyTorch reimplementation (the `sentence-transformers`/`setfit` libs are banned).* This is very feasible — the core is ~100 lines:
- Encode with BGE-M3 using its **native pooling (CLS token + L2 normalization)** — this is the key point for your question. Do *not* immediately bolt on a random head. BGE-M3's dense embedding is the `[CLS]` hidden state normalized to unit length (per the BAAI/bge-m3 card, huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3).
- Build pairs within each batch; loss is `torch.nn.CosineEmbeddingLoss` (or a SupCon-style InfoNCE) with label = +1 for same-intent, −1 for different. For **multi-label**, define pair positivity by label-set overlap (Jaccard) rather than exact equality — see the multi-label contrastive losses below.
- After a few contrastive epochs, freeze the body and fit a `sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression` (one-vs-rest) or a single `nn.Linear` head per class on the embeddings.

**FastFit (Yehudai & Bandel, NAACL 2024, arXiv:2404.12365; code github.com/IBM/fastfit).** A more recent alternative that integrates batch contrastive learning with a token-level similarity score, reporting a "3-20x improvement in training speed" over SetFit and better accuracy on many-class and multilingual benchmarks: FastFit-Large "outperformed SetFit by 2.1% and standard classifiers by 3.4%," scoring 83.2% average on 5-shot FewMany versus Mistral-7B at 59.6% and Llama-2-70B at 59.0%. The library is not on your allowed list, but its recipe (batch-contrastive + token-similarity) is reimplementable; for a 10-label problem the "many semantically similar classes" advantage is smaller than in its 77+-class target regime, so SetFit-style is the simpler first bet.

**Multi-label contrastive losses (the piece SetFit's original paper does not solve).** For overlapping label sets, use one of:
- **MulSupCon (Zhang & Wu, "Multi-Label Supervised Contrastive Learning", AAAI 2024, ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29619)** — adjusts contrastive weights by how much label overlap each sample shares with the anchor; the paper analyzes gradients to explain why this beats naive SupCon under multi-label.
- **Audibert et al. 2024, "Exploring Contrastive Learning for Long-Tailed Multi-Label Text Classification" (arXiv:2404.08720)** — introduces a contrastive loss that matches or beats standard losses on Micro-F1 and gives a significant Macro-F1 lift across three multi-label datasets, explicitly targeting long-tailed distributions (your exact setting).
- **Su et al. 2022, "An Effective Deployment of Contrastive Learning in Multi-label Text Classification" (arXiv:2212.00552)** — proposes five drop-in multi-label contrastive losses (Strict, Intra-label, Jaccard-Similarity, Jaccard-Similarity-Probability, Stepwise-Label). The Jaccard-Similarity Contrastive Loss (JSCL) is the natural pair-positivity definition for a SetFit reimplementation.

**Prompt-based methods (PET/iPET, LM-BFF).** PET (Schick & Schütze, arXiv:2001.07676; iPET arXiv:2009.07118; code github.com/timoschick/pet) and LM-BFF (Gao et al., ACL 2021, arXiv:2012.15723; code github.com/princeton-nlp/LM-BFF) reformulate classification as cloze/MLM prediction with verbalizers. These are strong at *true* few-shot (16–32 examples) but (a) require an MLM head, so they fit encoder-MLMs (XLM-R, mDeBERTa) rather than a retrieval embedder like BGE-M3; (b) extend awkwardly to multi-label (you would need per-label binary cloze prompts, multiplying forward passes); and (c) ADAPET/PET's advantage shrinks with 600–1900 examples, which is well past the regime where prompting dominates fine-tuning. **Verdict: lower priority than SetFit/LP-FT for your data scale**, but the per-label binary-cloze framing is a reasonable experiment for the two rare English classes specifically.

**Label-embedding / label-description-similarity heads.** Because BGE-M3 is a bilingual retriever, you can encode each of the 10 label *descriptions* (e.g., "customer asks about the price") in both languages and score an utterance by cosine similarity to each label embedding, then threshold — a zero-training baseline that also gives a strong *head initialization* for LP-FT. This is the multi-label analogue of the retrieval-based intent detection in Casanueva et al. 2020 ("Efficient intent detection with dual sentence encoders").

### 2. Fine-tuning instability on small data — the concrete recipe

Your finding that "LR mattered enormously for the 627-example English route" and "every model needed far more epochs than the plateau suggested" is exactly what this literature predicts. The consolidated recipe:

- **Use debiased AdamW, not BERTAdam.** Zhang et al. 2021 (arXiv:2006.05987; code github.com/asappresearch/revisit-bert-finetuning) show the omitted Adam bias-correction is a primary instability cause; HuggingFace's default `AdamW` already includes it, so confirm you are not disabling `correct_bias`. Mosbach et al. 2021 (arXiv:2006.04884) show bias correction acts as an implicit warmup by lowering the effective LR early — combine it with an **explicit linear warmup over the first ~10% of steps** followed by linear decay.
- **Re-initialize the top L transformer layers (L = 1–6, tune it) plus the pooler** (Zhang et al. 2021). The top layers of a pretrained encoder are over-specialized to the pretraining objective and provide a "detrimental initialization"; re-initializing them speeds convergence and improves final performance, and needs *fewer* iterations. This is a ~10-line change and is especially apt for BGE-M3, whose top layers are tuned for retrieval, not classification.
- **Train longer with early stopping.** Both papers stress that standard 3-epoch recipes under-train; run to near-zero training loss with early stopping on a *robust* dev metric (see §5). You have already observed this.
- **Small-LR + bias-correction is Mosbach's "simple but hard-to-beat baseline."** Given your English route liked 1e-4/5e-5, the literature suggests the *instability* (not the optimum) is what LR was fixing; adding warmup + re-init should let a smaller, safer LR reach the same or better dev F1 with less variance.
- **Regularizers to try, in rough priority:** **LLRD (layer-wise LR decay)** — apply a decay factor (~0.9–0.95) so lower layers move less; **Mixout (Lee et al. 2020)** — stochastically replace fine-tuned weights with pretrained ones, shown to stabilize sub-few-thousand-example fine-tuning; **R-Drop / Noise-Stability Regularization (arXiv:2107.04835)** and **Component-Wise Gradient Norm Clipping (arXiv:2210.10325)** as lighter alternatives. Note one caveat: on *extreme* data scarcity, Re-init and Mixout have been reported to fail to help (arXiv:2205.01307), so treat these as tunables validated on your dev, not guaranteed wins. **Child-tuning (Xu et al. 2021)** and **SWA/checkpoint averaging** (you already do logit averaging) round out the toolkit.
- **LP-FT (Kumar et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.10054).** First train only the head (linear probe) to convergence, then unfreeze and fine-tune everything. The theory: fine-tuning with a random head distorts good pretrained features; a probed head means the subsequent full fine-tune needs only small feature updates. Empirically, verbatim from the paper: "LP-FT outperforms both fine-tuning and linear probing on the above datasets (1% better ID, 10% better OOD than full fine-tuning)"; across 10 distribution-shift datasets, full fine-tuning alone gave "on average 2% higher accuracy ID but 7% lower accuracy OOD than linear probing." **This is doubly relevant to you:** (a) BGE-M3's features are strong (so distortion is costly), and (b) your public test has a different zh/en ratio (~50/50) than train/dev (~75/25) — a genuine distribution shift, exactly where LP-FT's OOD advantage shows up. LP-FT is nearly free (LP step is fast) and is my #1 recommended experiment.

### 3. Cross-lingual data augmentation: translate-train, not back-translation

**Translate-train is well-established and higher-value than back-translation in resource-imbalanced bilingual setups.** In XTREME (Hu et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.11080), translate-train (translating training data into the target language and training there) substantially beats zero-shot cross-lingual transfer: per Google Research / Hu et al. 2020, "On the XNLI task, mBERT scored 65.4 in the zero shot transfer setting, and 74.0 when using translated training data." On MultiATIS++ intent classification, "Trans-Train SOTA" and "Translate-Intent" rows reach ~94–96% intent accuracy, competitive with target-language training (CrossAligner & Co, arXiv:2203.09982).

**Why this is different from your failed back-translation.** Back-translation (EN→ZH→EN) produces paraphrases of your *existing* 627 English examples — no new lexical/semantic content, and it doubles the count of the same distribution, which is why it plausibly failed twice. Translate-train (ZH→EN on your ~1900 Chinese examples) injects **genuinely new content and label combinations** the English model has never seen, roughly tripling effective English data with new information. Labels transfer directly because intent labels are utterance-level (no span alignment needed, unlike slot filling) — this is the easy case for label projection.

**Caveats from the literature and how to handle them:**
- **Translationese / artifacts.** Machine-translated text has detectable statistical artifacts and can mismatch the target's natural distribution. Mitigations: (a) **tag** translated examples with a learned indicator or a distinct token, or (b) **down-weight** them in the loss, or (c) use them only for the LP / contrastive-pretraining stage and fine-tune the final epochs on genuine English only.
- **Domain/cultural mismatch.** Sales phrasing for `Too_Expensive`/`Compare_Competitor` may translate awkwardly; spot-check translations for these two classes specifically.
- **Mixing ratio.** Start conservative (e.g., genuine:translated ≈ 1:1 or 1:2) and tune on dev; do not let translated data dominate.
- **Offline MT within your allowed packages.** MarianMT (`Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-zh-en`), NLLB-200 (`facebook/nllb-200-distilled-600M`), or M2M100 all run via `transformers` offline at *training* time (translation happens during dev, never at inference), so this respects your no-network inference constraint. NLLB-distilled-600M gives markedly better zh→en quality than small Marian models and fits your budget.

**The multilingual-joint alternative (likely simpler and comparable).** Because BGE-M3 is multilingual, two training-free-of-MT options are strong baselines against translate-train:
- **Joint training:** train the English route on en + zh data together (the shared multilingual encoder transfers Chinese supervision to English).
- **Sequential fine-tuning:** fine-tune on the abundant Chinese data first, then continue on English (intermediate-task transfer; Phang et al. 2018 show a large intermediate task stabilizes small-data fine-tuning).

These test the same hypothesis (English is data-starved and can borrow from Chinese) *without* MT noise. Given translate-train adds translationese risk, I recommend running **zh→en sequential fine-tuning and joint training first**, and only reaching for MT translate-train if those under-perform — they are cheaper and cleaner.

### 4. Rare-class techniques for multi-label beyond ASL / weighted-BCE / thresholding

- **Distribution-Balanced Loss (Wu et al. 2020, ECCV Spotlight, arXiv/ECVA; code github.com/wutong16/DistributionBalancedLoss).** Two modifications to BCE: (1) **rebalanced weighting that accounts for label co-occurrence** (standard re-weighting over-counts frequent labels that co-occur with rare ones — directly relevant since your labels co-occur), and (2) **negative-tolerant regularization** to stop the flood of negative labels from over-suppressing rare positives. Designed for long-tailed multi-label.
- **Huang et al. 2021, "Balancing Methods for Multi-label Text Classification with Long-Tailed Class Distribution" (EMNLP 2021, aclanthology 2021.emnlp-main.643; code github.com/Roche/BalancedLossNLP).** This is the **most directly applicable paper** — it ports DB-Loss and class-balanced focal loss to *NLP multi-label text* and introduces a novel **CB-NTR (class-balanced negative-tolerant regularization)** loss. Their experiments used "a general domain dataset with 90 labels (Reuters-21578)" (rarest label "occurs in only 5 documents," ~1.24 labels/doc) "and a domain-specific dataset from PubMed with 18,211 labels." The tail-class gains are dramatic: per the paper, "BCE has the lowest performance for the Reuters dataset with total macro-F1 score of 47 and tail F1 scores of 0… DB provides the biggest improvement in tail class assignment; the tail micro-F1 score gains 21.49 from FL and 25.81 from CB." The repo is plain PyTorch and drops into your existing BCE training loop.
- **Class-Balanced Loss (Cui et al. 2019, arXiv:1901.05555; code github.com/richardaecn/class-balanced-loss).** Re-weights by the "effective number of samples" `(1−β^n)/(1−β)`, a smoothed interpolation between no-reweighting and inverse-frequency. Loss-agnostic (works with sigmoid/BCE). This is the theoretically-grounded upgrade to your current `sqrt((N−N_k)/N_k)` weighting.
- **Logit adjustment (Menon et al. 2021, arXiv:2007.07314).** Add a per-class offset `τ·log(prior)` to logits (at training or post-hoc). Post-hoc logit adjustment is essentially free and composes with your per-class threshold tuning — worth a quick test as a cheaper cousin of threshold search.
- **sigmoidF1 (Bénédict et al. 2022, arXiv:2108.10566; ICLR/OpenReview gvSHaaD2wQ).** A smooth, differentiable surrogate for F1 that optimizes label prediction *and* label counts jointly, benchmarked to beat cross-entropy/focal on multi-label F1 metrics. Since you are graded on Macro/Micro-F1 against fixed thresholds, directly optimizing an F1 surrogate is well-motivated — but note it can be finicky to tune (temperature β, offset η) and did not dominate on every dataset/metric in the paper, so treat as a secondary experiment.
- **Targeted rare-class tactics:** metric-learning/prototype heads for `Too_Expensive`/`Compare_Competitor` (LSPCL, ScienceDirect S0950705124015211, builds label-specific prototype repositories); and translate-train aimed *specifically* at generating more English examples of these two classes.

**Recommendation ordering for loss:** replace ASL with **DB-Loss / CB-NTR (Huang 2021 repo)** as the primary lever, keep per-class thresholding (your biggest cheap win), and test post-hoc logit adjustment and sigmoidF1 as add-ons.

### 5. SOTA / leaderboard search — methods to borrow, not just datasets

- **NLU++ (Casanueva et al., Findings of NAACL 2022, arXiv:2204.13021).** The closest analog to your task: real-world, **multi-label** intent detection with small data and a modular intent ontology, evaluated with **K-fold cross-validation** (their explicit setup) — the model-selection protocol you should adopt for your noisy English slice. Their strong baselines use **dual sentence encoders (ConveRT/MLP heads)** rather than vanilla BERT fine-tuning, corroborating the embedding-model-as-classifier direction. Multi³NLU++ (arXiv:2212.10455) extends it multilingually and multi-intent.
- **Few-shot multi-label intent detection (Hou et al. 2020, arXiv:2010.05256).** Directly on-topic for multi-label intent with few examples — worth mining for its threshold/meta-learning tactics.
- **MixATIS / MixSNIPS (Qin et al. 2020)** are the classic multi-intent benchmarks but are *synthetic* (concatenated single-intent utterances), so borrow methods, not expectations; NLU++'s critique is that real multi-intent data (like yours) is harder and less repetitive.
- **BANKING77 / CLINC / DialoGLUE few-shot intent work** (Casanueva et al. 2020, "Efficient intent detection with dual sentence encoders"; QAID arXiv; FastFit) consistently find that **contrastive/dual-encoder heads beat plain classification heads** in low-data intent detection — the central transferable lesson.
- **General text-classification reality check:** "Are We Really Making Much Progress in Text Classification?" (arXiv:2204.03954) and "Text Classification in the LLM Era" (arXiv:2502.11830) both find well-tuned encoder fine-tuning remains competitive with far larger models, and the latter uses FastFit for its few-shot fine-tuning — reassurance that your encoder-route strategy is the right ballpark and that squeezing the recipe (§1–§4) is where the gains are.

## Recommendations

Given your dev-iteration budget (single RTX 3080 Ti, 1–5 min/run), run experiments in this order; each is cheap and the early ones are the highest-expected-value:

**Stage 1 — fix the optimization (do first, ~1 day):**
1. **LP-FT on BGE-M3** (Kumar 2022): linear-probe the head to convergence, then full fine-tune. Highest expected value, near-free, and matches your train/public-test distribution shift.
2. **Stability recipe on the English route:** confirm debiased AdamW, add linear warmup (10% steps), re-initialize the top 2–6 layers + pooler (Zhang 2021 — sweep L), and train to near-zero train loss with early stopping. Expect the English route's LR sensitivity to collapse.
3. **Switch model selection to 5-fold CV on the English data** with multi-seed (≥5 seeds) averaging (Dodge 2020, NLU++ protocol). This stops you chasing noise on the 117-example dev slice — a prerequisite for trusting every subsequent result.

**Stage 2 — fix the rare classes (~1 day):**
4. **Replace ASL with Distribution-Balanced / CB-NTR loss** (Huang 2021 repo, plain PyTorch). Keep per-class threshold tuning. Test **post-hoc logit adjustment** as a free add-on.
5. **Class-balanced (effective-number) weighting** (Cui 2019) as a fallback if DB-Loss doesn't help.

**Stage 3 — fix the data imbalance (~1–2 days):**
6. **zh→en sequential fine-tuning and multilingual joint training** first (no MT, cleanest test of the "borrow from Chinese" hypothesis).
7. If those under-deliver, **translate-train** the ~1900 Chinese examples to English with NLLB-200-distilled-600M, tag/down-weight translated examples, mix ~1:1, targeting the two rare classes especially.

**Stage 4 — paradigm and model swaps (~2 days, only if Stages 1–3 haven't cleared Challenge on public test):**
8. **SetFit-style contrastive fine-tuning reimplemented in plain PyTorch** on BGE-M3's native CLS+norm embeddings, using a Jaccard-overlap pair definition (Su 2022 JSCL) or MulSupCon, then a logistic-regression head.
9. **Try BGE-large-en-v1.5 / BGE-large-zh-v1.5** as language-specialized route models under the identical recipe; **multilingual-e5-large** as the same-architecture comparison (with `query:` prefix).

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** if Stage 1 alone lifts English dev Macro-F1 from ~0.83–0.84 toward ~0.86 (Chinese parity) and public-test Micro-F1 clears 0.83, you have hit Challenge and can stop. If the rare two classes remain the bottleneck after Stage 2, prioritize Stage 3 translate-train targeted at them. If nothing in Stages 1–3 moves public test despite dev gains, the problem is model-selection overfitting to dev — double down on CV (step 3) before touching architecture.

## Caveats
- **Compliance risk is real and model-specific.** jina-v3, Qwen3-Embedding, and mE5-instruct were trained on classification data; under a strict reading of your rule, avoid them. Qwen3-Embedding *also* violates your transformers 4.50.0 pin. Confirm each model's `trust_remote_code` behavior loads under 4.50.0 before relying on it.
- **Some stability regularizers fail under extreme scarcity.** Re-init and Mixout have been reported ineffective on very small (few-hundred-example) sets in at least one study (arXiv:2205.01307), so validate them on your dev rather than assuming gains; 627 examples is borderline.
- **sigmoidF1 and prompt-based (PET) methods are secondary bets** — sigmoidF1 is tuning-sensitive and did not dominate every dataset in its paper; PET fits MLM encoders, not BGE-M3, and its few-shot edge shrinks at your data scale.
- **Translationese is a documented risk**; treat translate-train output as noisy auxiliary data (tag/down-weight), and hand-check the two rare-class translations.
